# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

FAANG / big-tech recruiters and technical sourcers reviewing Huzaifa Naroo's portfolio profile. Two viewing modes to design for, in this order:

- **5-second scan**: a sourcer skims for legible credentials, scale signals (100K+ users, named employers, stack), and "is this person worth a screen?"
- **5-minute read**: a hiring manager or peer engineer dives deeper into specific projects, looking for evidence of real systems thinking, range, and originality.

The job-to-be-done is a three-step funnel: **read → trust → reach out** (email/DM about a role). Every section must earn its place in that arc.

## Product Purpose

The recruiter-facing content site for a Mario-themed portfolio. A separate `game-mode` exists at the same domain and is the project's differentiator — a fully playable Mario level where pipes link to GitHub / LinkedIn / Email / Resume. The content site lives next to it.

Success looks like a recruiter who (1) understands within seconds that this person ships, (2) trusts the work after reading two projects, and (3) closes the tab quoting one specific detail (the Mario mode, a project, a turn of phrase) when forwarding the profile internally.

The content site does not have to carry the differentiation alone — the game mode does that. The content site's job is to be senior-grade craft that doesn't squander the trust the game mode earned.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **sharp, playful, undeniable.**

Aesthetic lane: **game-cartridge nostalgia, rendered modern.** Reference points are Teenage Engineering, Panic Inc., Are.na clubs — products that acknowledge retro-tactile cues (CRT scanlines, 16-bit grids, sprite-style punctuation, monospace runs) but execute them with current typographic and motion craft, not cosplay.

Voice: confident, specific, dryly funny when it earns it. First-person where it sharpens the writing; never breathless or self-promotional. A recruiter should read three sentences and think "this person is a senior engineer who happens to have taste," not "this is a junior trying to seem fun."

## Anti-references

- **Dev-portfolio template aesthetics.** Dark navy + neon-green-terminal is banned even when it's the easy answer for "engineer."
- **Hero-metric template.** Big number / small label / supporting stats / gradient accent. The "100K+ users" line exists in the data; it must be earned in copy, not stamped as a hero stat.
- **Identical project card grids.** Three-up icon-heading-text rows are out. Each project gets a layout that fits the project — varied weight, varied scale, varied composition.
- **Cosplay retro.** Pixel font everywhere, CRT filter on every block, "Press Start 2P" as headline type. The Mario reference is a wink, not a costume.
- **Cream + serif design-portfolio look.** Reads as designer, not engineer; wrong audience signal.

## Design Principles

1. **Playfulness is the proof, not the costume.** The Mario game mode is the differentiator; the content site references it with restraint. One or two earned moments of game-mode personality beats sprinkling sprites everywhere.
2. **Senior craft above all.** Originality must never confuse a 5-second scanner. If a recruiter has to puzzle out the navigation or the hierarchy, the playfulness has cost more than it earned.
3. **Show, don't list.** Avoid the hero-metric template and identical card grids. Let each project's actual specifics — the system, the constraint, the outcome — carry the weight that templates would otherwise paper over.
4. **Scan-then-stay.** Every section answers the 5-second question first (one legible line of signal) and rewards the 5-minute reader second (depth, links, specifics). Never one without the other.
5. **Two loud moments, the rest earned design furniture.** The pipe-warp transition (entry into game-mode) and the 1UP toast (contact submit) carry the headline cartridge moments. Beyond those, NES motifs — Mario ground bands as section dividers, ?-block sprites as numerals on numbered lists, coin discs anchoring counted numbers, ambient drifting clouds — earn their place by serving the content directly (a coin next to a counted thing; a ?-block next to an enumerated thing). Never sprite-for-sprite's-sake; never type set in Press Start 2P.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor: contrast, focus rings, semantic landmarks, alt text on every visual.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected for every non-essential animation, including any retro-tactile flourishes (scanlines, sprite bobs, transition effects).
- Keyboard-navigable end to end. Recruiters using screen readers or keyboard-only must reach every project, link, and contact path.
- No information conveyed by color alone (status states, project categories, etc.).
